01

One app, six games

Switch on the games you play. The rest stay out of your way.

Dota 2

Follows your match live: last hits at every 5 minutes against your own averages, each death and the gold it cost, item timings and your goals. Plus history, heroes, the meta and builds.

Deadlock

Your overview, matches with full scoreboards, heroes, the hero and item meta, builds, and Deadlock Live: the top live matches with the streamers on each hero.

Counter-Strike 2

Round by round as you play: kills, damage, headshots, weapons, what you bought and how each round ended. Win rates by side, buy and map.

Overwatch

Career stats by mode, role and hero from your public profile, the hero meta with your own win rate beside it, and your sessions and ranks over time.

PUBG

From PUBG's official API: season and ranked stats, your recent matches with your squad, and a review of each one: where you landed, how every knock and death happened, and blue zone damage.

Call of Duty

Black Ops 7, Warzone and Modern Warfare 4. Activision shares no stats with apps, so you log each match in one line, and TheTracker times your sessions and reviews your log.

02

Made for while you play

  • In-game overlayA small see-through window over Dota 2 that counts down to runes, lotuses and the stack pull, and shows why you died while you wait to respawn.
  • Match reviewsEvery Dota 2, Deadlock, PUBG and Call of Duty match checked against your own games, with what went wrong and a list of things to work on across your last matches.
  • Pro settingsVideo, audio and hotkey presets for Dota 2, Deadlock, CS2 and Call of Duty, built from pros' published settings. Every file is backed up first, and one click puts it back.
  • Connection checkDuring a match it tells you whether lag comes from your home network, your internet provider or a busy line. Game mode can also slow background uploads while you play.
  • TodayOne page with today's and this week's record, streaks and your last match in every game.
  • Compare and sharePut a friend's public numbers next to yours, and save your form or a match as a picture.
  • LeaderboardYour best recorded games, and a shared board if you sign in.
  • NotificationsA Windows notification when a Dota 2 or CS2 match is saved, if you want one.
03

Plays fair

TheTracker never reads game memory. Dota 2 and CS2 send your own match state to the app through Valve's Game State Integration, and history comes from public sources like OpenDota, the Deadlock API, OverFast and PUBG's official API. Call of Duty has no public stats, so those matches are the ones you log. It shows nothing about other players that the game doesn't already show you.

04

Questions

Anything else? Ask on GitHub.

How do I link my account?

Sign in with Steam on Steam's own page, so the app never sees your password. That links Dota 2 and Deadlock. Overwatch connects with your BattleTag (your profile needs to be public), and PUBG with your in-game name.

Why do I log Call of Duty matches myself?

Activision doesn't share match stats with apps. Logging takes one line (game, mode, result, kills and deaths, then Enter), and everything else, from sessions to reviews, is worked out from it.

Does it record games when it's closed?

No, only matches played while it's running. It can start with Windows and wait quietly in the tray.

Why is there no Valorant?

Riot only shares match data with apps it has reviewed and approved, with each player signing in through Riot. TheTracker doesn't have that yet, so it doesn't pretend to.

Where is my data kept?

On your PC, in %APPDATA%\TheTracker. Settings → General can back it up and restore it.

How does it update?

It updates itself, and checks the signature of every update before installing it.
